# Community validation workbook

**Current evidence status:** synthetic campus only, at the user's request. No interviews, school partnership, real pathway survey, participant study, or measured community impact has been supplied. Palos Verdes Peninsula is a potential pilot context, not a mapped or verified partner.

## Research context

The [CDC's barriers overview](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-inclusion/barriers/index.html) identifies steps and curbs as examples of physical barriers to mobility. This supports the general problem; it does not establish a barrier at a particular school or validate AccessPath.

The [U.S. Access Board's accessible-route guide](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-4-accessible-routes/) covers connected routes and several route components. AccessPath's limited attributes and chosen slope filter are not a legal compliance assessment.

## Collect evidence respectfully

Invite voluntary participation and explain that this is a student prototype. Offer an accessible format and allow participants to skip questions or withdraw. Ask about routes and information needs, not diagnoses. Do not ask someone to use a dangerous or unusable route to prove a barrier. Follow school/guardian requirements when applicable. Keep names and contact details out of the public repository and obtain explicit permission before quoting anyone.

## Interview guide — blank until a real conversation

1. Which journeys are hardest to plan, and what information is missing?
2. How do you currently learn about stairs, entrances, or temporary closures?
3. When information is uncertain, what would you want the app to say?
4. Which route details matter to you that this prototype does not capture?
5. Would text directions, a map, or both help? What makes either difficult?

Record participant code, consent scope, date, actual notes, and permitted anonymized quotation. **Do not fill responses with hypothetical people.** Distinguish verbatim quotation from the student's interpretation.

## Prototype task session

Ask a willing participant to compare two demo routes, identify a known stair segment, explain an unknown segment, find the text itinerary, and submit a synthetic obstacle report. Explain the campus is fictional before starting. Provide keyboard-only and zoomed-text options without requiring them.

For each task record completion or difficulty, the observed interaction, the participant's own feedback, and the change made. Do not equate automated tests with user research or announce an accessibility certification from one session.
